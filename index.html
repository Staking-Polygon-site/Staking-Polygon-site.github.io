What it is

What staking Polygon means

Delegating POL to validators

Staking Polygon means delegating your POL tokens to a validator, a node operator that helps secure the Polygon network. In return, the validator earns rewards and passes a share on to the people who delegated to it, after taking its commission.

You keep ownership

Delegating does not transfer your tokens to the validator. Your POL stays under your control and is bonded to the validator, and you can start withdrawing it later. The validator never receives your keys.

Delegate vs hold

Delegating compared with holding

Both keep your POL exposed to its market price. Delegation adds rewards, along with a wait before you can withdraw.

In practice

Delegate the part of your POL you are happy to leave bonded, and keep the rest liquid for gas and other uses.

How to stake

How to stake on Polygon

Delegate, earn, withdraw

Each stage is an on-chain transaction that you sign yourself. Nothing is delegated until you confirm.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and choose a validator to delegate to.
  2. Enter the amount of POL and approve the token if asked.
  3. Sign the delegation transaction and wait for confirmation.
  4. Claim or restake rewards when you choose to.
  5. To exit, start unbonding, wait out the withdrawal period, then withdraw your POL.

Delegated POL is not available immediately after you request to unstake. The unbonding wait must finish before the tokens are released.

Fees & gas

What staking on Polygon costs

Gas

Staking on Polygon is managed by contracts on Ethereum, so delegating, claiming and withdrawing are Ethereum transactions paid in ETH. See how gas works.

Validator commission

Validators keep a share of the rewards they earn before passing the rest to delegators. Compare commissions when you choose, since they affect what reaches your wallet.

Keep ETH for gas

Holding POL does not pay for these transactions. Keep some ETH in the same wallet or you will not be able to delegate, claim or withdraw.

Security

Staying safe

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. The interface prepares transactions; it never holds your POL or your keys. Read the Polygon docs for how staking works on the network.

Before you sign

  • Verify the staking contract and validator before approving.
  • Pick a validator after checking its commission and reliability.
  • Delegate only what you can leave untouched through the unbonding wait.
  • Validators can be penalized and contracts can hold bugs, so risk is never zero.
FAQ

Staking Polygon FAQ

What is staking Polygon?

Staking Polygon means delegating POL to a validator that helps secure the Polygon network. The validator earns rewards and shares them with its delegators after commission. You sign each step from your own wallet.

How do I earn rewards when I delegate POL?

Rewards accrue while your POL is delegated to a validator, and you claim or restake them when you choose. The amount depends on the validator and network conditions, and this page does not state a rate. Gas is needed for each transaction.

Is there a wait to withdraw delegated POL?

Yes. After you start unbonding, the tokens stay locked for the unbonding period before you can withdraw them. You then pay gas to complete the withdrawal.

Does the validator take custody of my POL?

No. Delegation is non-custodial: your tokens stay bonded under your control and the validator does not receive your keys. The interface never holds your funds either.

Which gas token do I need to stake on Polygon?

Staking is managed by contracts on Ethereum, so delegating, claiming and withdrawing are paid for in ETH. Keep some ETH in your wallet in addition to your POL.

What are the risks of staking POL?

The POL price can change, validators can be penalized, and contracts can contain bugs. Your funds are also unavailable during the unbonding wait. Delegate only an amount you can afford to leave bonded.
