The practical guide

How to Stake MATIC

To stake MATIC (now POL), fund a self-custody wallet, connect it to the official Polygon staking dashboard, delegate your tokens to a validator you have vetted on commission and uptime, then claim or restake the rewards it pays out.

    Before You Stake: Wallet and Tokens

    Staking MATIC happens on Ethereum mainnet, not on the Polygon PoS chain itself, because the staking contracts live on L1. That single fact dictates everything about preparation: you need a self-custody wallet, your tokens as POL rather than legacy MATIC, and enough ETH on hand to cover the gas each staking action costs. Skipping any of the three is the most common reason a first delegation stalls halfway through signing.

    Preparing a wallet

    Install a self-custody wallet such as MetaMask or pair a hardware device following the Ledger setup guide, then make sure it is connected to the Ethereum mainnet network. Back up the recovery phrase offline before funding it. A hardware wallet signing through MetaMask gives you the delegation convenience of a browser extension with keys that never touch the internet.

    Getting MATIC or POL

    If you hold legacy MATIC, migrate it to POL first — the current staking contracts expect POL. Acquire the token on an exchange and withdraw it to your wallet, or bridge it to Ethereum mainnet if it currently sits on another chain. Keep 0.02-0.05 ETH spare for gas; delegating, claiming and unbonding are each separate on-chain transactions.

    Choosing a Validator

    Because most people stake as delegators rather than running infrastructure, the validator you point your stake at is the single decision that matters most. Its commission eats into every reward payout, its uptime determines whether you earn full rewards, and its checkpoint-signing performance affects both. Treat the validator list as a shortlist to research, not a menu to pick the top row from. The table below is the checklist to run against each candidate before you commit a single token.
    What to checkGood signWhy it matters
    Commission rate5-10%, stable over timeComes straight off your rewards; a jump signals the operator may hike it again
    Uptime history99%+ over several monthsDowntime means missed rewards and, in severe cases, penalties for delegators
    Checkpoint performanceHigh signed-checkpoint ratioPolygon rewards validators per checkpoint; missed ones reduce the pool you share
    Total stake / saturationHealthy but not maxed outOver-concentrated validators centralize the network and may cap new delegations
    Track record / identityNamed operator, public historyAn anonymous operator with no history is harder to hold accountable

    Reading commission and uptime

    A 10% commission on a 4% gross yield leaves you 3.6% net — small differences compound over a year. Cross-check the dashboard's figures against a block explorer so you are reading on-chain reality rather than a self-reported number. A validator advertising 0% commission is often subsidizing growth temporarily and can raise the rate once it has your stake.

    Why checkpoint performance matters

    Polygon validators submit checkpoints to Ethereum, and rewards are distributed per successfully signed checkpoint. A validator that frequently misses checkpoints earns less for the same stake, which directly shrinks the reward pool its delegators split. Favor operators with a high signed-checkpoint ratio over the entire recent window, not just the last day.

    Delegating Step by Step

    With a funded wallet and a chosen validator, the delegation itself is a short, repeatable sequence. Follow it in order — the failures people actually hit come from signing before the amount is confirmed or from running out of ETH for gas mid-flow, not from anything exotic.
    • Open the official Polygon staking dashboard at staking.polygon.technology and connect your wallet.
    • Confirm the dashboard sees your POL balance and that you are on Ethereum mainnet.
    • Search for your vetted validator by name and open its detail page to re-check commission and uptime.
    • Enter the amount of POL to delegate, leaving spare ETH for gas on this and later actions.
    • Approve the token-spend transaction, then sign the delegation transaction and wait for both to confirm.
    • Verify the new delegation appears under your validator with the correct staked amount.

    The two-transaction pattern

    First delegations usually require two signatures: an approval that lets the staking contract move your POL, and the delegation itself. Each costs L1 gas, so a burst of high network activity can make the second transaction pricier than you planned. If the approval succeeds but you cancel the delegation, your tokens stay in your wallet — no stake is created until the second signature confirms.

    Where Rewards Come From and How to Claim

    Staking rewards on Polygon come from protocol emissions and are distributed to validators per checkpoint, which then pass the delegators' share along minus commission. Rewards accrue continuously but do not compound on their own — they sit as a claimable balance until you act. You have two practical choices each cycle, and the table lays out when each one makes sense.
    ActionWhat it doesWhen it makes sense
    Claim to walletMoves accrued rewards to your spendable POL balanceWhen you want the income now or plan to use the tokens elsewhere
    Restake (compound)Adds accrued rewards back to your delegationWhen maximizing long-run yield and gas cost is small relative to the reward
    Let it accrueLeaves rewards claimable without a transactionWhen accrued rewards are still smaller than the gas to claim them

    Claim versus restake

    Claiming gives you liquid POL you can spend or sell; restaking folds rewards back into your stake so the next cycle earns on a larger base. Because each action costs gas, restaking a tiny reward can cost more than it adds. A common rhythm is to let rewards build until they comfortably exceed the gas, then restake in one transaction.

    Reward cadence

    There is no fixed payday — rewards stream in as checkpoints are submitted and you realize them whenever you choose to claim or restake. Many delegators batch this monthly or quarterly to keep gas overhead proportional to the amount being moved rather than paying L1 fees on trivial sums every few days.

    Unbonding and Withdrawing

    Exiting is where the only real waiting happens, and it catches people who assume staking is as instant as a swap. Unstaking on Polygon is a two-phase process: you first unbond, which starts a checkpoint-based cooldown of roughly three to four days, and only after that window can you withdraw the tokens back to your wallet. Plan the timing before you commit funds you might need quickly.

    Starting the unbond

    From the staking dashboard, select your delegation and choose to unstake the amount you want to exit. Signing that transaction removes the stake from the validator and begins the cooldown clock. Your tokens are neither earning rewards nor transferable during this window — they are in limbo until the cooldown completes.

    The 3-4 day wait and final withdrawal

    Polygon ties the cooldown to checkpoints (roughly 80 checkpoints, about three to four days in practice) rather than a fixed timer, so the exact wait flexes with network conditions. Once it elapses, return to the dashboard and sign a separate withdraw transaction to move the POL back into your spendable balance. If you need liquidity faster than this, a liquid-staking receipt token sold on the open market is the usual workaround — at the cost of market and peg risk.

    Delegate Directly vs Liquid Stake

    Direct delegation is the path this guide walks through: you keep control, choose your own validator, and accept the unbonding wait. Liquid staking is the alternative — a protocol stakes on your behalf and mints a receipt token you can trade or use in DeFi while the underlying POL stays locked. Neither is strictly better; they trade control and simplicity against liquidity and extra risk.
    FactorDirect delegationLiquid staking
    CustodyYour own wallet holds the stakeA smart contract holds it; you hold a receipt token
    Exit speed~3-4 day unbonding cooldownSell the receipt token instantly on the market
    Validator choiceYou pick and monitor itThe protocol chooses the validator set
    Extra riskSlashing and validator downtimeAdds smart-contract and peg/de-peg risk
    Capital useLocked until withdrawnReceipt token stays usable across DeFi

    Picking the model for your goals

    Choose direct delegation when you want maximum control and are comfortable waiting out the cooldown to exit. Choose liquid staking when ongoing liquidity matters more than avoiding an extra contract layer. The decision is about how much you value an instant exit and composable collateral against keeping the simplest possible risk surface. If you are staking a long-term position you have no plans to touch, the cooldown is a non-issue and direct delegation keeps your risk surface as small as it gets.

    Fees, Net Yield and Common Mistakes

    A clean delegation can still disappoint if you only looked at the headline APR. Three costs sit between gross emissions and what actually lands in your wallet: the validator's commission, the Ethereum L1 gas on every action, and the opportunity cost of the unbonding cooldown. Reasoning about net yield rather than advertised yield is what separates a stake that quietly compounds from one that bleeds value to fees. Just as important is sidestepping the handful of avoidable mistakes that trip up first-time delegators, each of which costs either money or time.
    • Delegating a stake so small that claim and restake gas routinely eats the rewards it earns.
    • Picking a 0% commission validator without checking whether that rate is a temporary promotion.
    • Forgetting to leave spare ETH, so the delegation or withdrawal transaction fails on gas.
    • Chasing the single highest advertised APR instead of the best uptime-to-commission balance.
    • Starting an unbond right before you need the funds, then being surprised by the 3-4 day wait.

    Calculating net yield

    Start from the gross APR, subtract the commission as a percentage of rewards, then subtract annualized gas for however often you plan to claim or restake. A 4% gross yield under a 10% commission is 3.6% before gas; on a small stake, frequent claiming can push the real figure meaningfully lower. The larger your stake, the less gas matters as a share of rewards, which is why net yield improves with position size.

    Keeping records

    Note which validator holds each delegation, the commission at the time you staked, and the dates of any claims or restakes. Commissions can change, and a validator that was excellent a year ago may have drifted on uptime. A quick quarterly review against a block explorer catches a deteriorating operator long before it meaningfully dents your returns, and makes the eventual tax or accounting picture far simpler to reconstruct.

    Frequently asked questions

    A closer look at the details.

    Do I stake MATIC or POL now?
    You stake POL. The network migrated its staking token from MATIC to POL, and the current contracts expect POL, so legacy MATIC holders should migrate first before delegating.
    How much MATIC do I need to start staking?
    The protocol minimum is as low as 1 POL, but because every action costs Ethereum L1 gas, very small stakes are inefficient — the fees can outweigh the rewards. A stake large enough that rewards clearly exceed claim gas is the practical floor.
    Why do I need ETH to stake MATIC?
    The staking contracts run on Ethereum mainnet, so delegating, claiming and unbonding are each L1 transactions that cost gas paid in ETH. Keep roughly 0.02-0.05 ETH spare so no step stalls for lack of gas.
    How long does unstaking take?
    Unbonding triggers a checkpoint-based cooldown of about three to four days (around 80 checkpoints) before you can sign the final withdrawal. The exact wait flexes with network conditions rather than being a fixed timer.
    Do staking rewards compound automatically?
    No. Rewards accrue as a claimable balance but do not auto-compound. You manually claim them to your wallet or restake them into your delegation, and each action is a separate gas-paying transaction.
    How do I pick a good validator?
    Check its commission rate, long-term uptime, and checkpoint-signing performance, and favor a named operator with a public track record. Cross-check the dashboard's figures against a block explorer rather than trusting self-reported numbers.
    Can I lose my staked MATIC?
    Your principal can be eroded if your validator is penalized for extended downtime or misbehavior, and liquid-staking adds smart-contract and peg risk on top. Choosing a high-uptime validator with a solid history sharply reduces the odds.
